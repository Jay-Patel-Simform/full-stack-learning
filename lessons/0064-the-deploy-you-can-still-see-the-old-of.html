The line you have been reading past

It has been in every curl output in this workspace:

cache-control: public, max-age=0, s-maxage=300

Three instructions, and they are aimed at different machines:

PartWho obeys itWhat it says
publicEveryoneAny cache may store this, including shared ones. The opposite is private: browser only, never a CDN.
max-age=0The browserTreat your copy as stale immediately. Before reusing it, ask the server whether it changed.
s-maxage=300The shared cache (Cloudflare, in front of Render)For five minutes, answer from your copy without asking Render at all.

Two caches sit between your build and your user, and they take separate orders. max-age is the one in the user’s laptop; s-maxage is the one in the datacentre near them. Nearly every caching bug is someone tuning one of those numbers while thinking about the other.

So what does s-maxage=300 actually cost you? After a deploy finishes, a user can still be served the previous index.html for up to five minutes. Not because their browser is stale — max-age=0 prevents that — but because the CDN between them and Render is answering from a copy it took before you pushed. That is your staleness window, and it is a number you chose by not choosing it.

Your site has two kinds of file, and they want opposite things

Look at what Vite actually built. Fetch your own homepage and read the script tag:

/index.html/assets/index-D1HMFRqX.js
Name after a rebuildAlways /index.htmlNew hash if the bytes changed, same hash if not
Can this name mean new bytes?Yes — that is its whole jobNo. Different bytes get a different name
Right cachingNever reuse without askingReuse for as long as you like
What it is sent todaymax-age=0, s-maxage=300max-age=0, s-maxage=300 — identical

That is the gap. index.html is the pointer; the hashed files are the contents. The pointer changes and must be re-read. The contents are addressed by their own hash, so re-checking them is asking a question whose answer cannot ever have changed.

what that costs you, measured
I sent your bundle’s ETag back with an If-None-Match header. The response was HTTP/2 304 — no body, so the megabyte of JavaScript is not re-downloaded. Good. But it is still a network round trip per file, on every page load, before the browser will run a byte of a file it already has. On your home wifi that is a few milliseconds. On a phone on mobile data it is a few hundred, multiplied by every hashed file in the build. The waste is not bandwidth, it is latency, and it is paid on the load where the user is already waiting to see something.

The fix: one more headers entry

Same headers: block as lesson 63, one entry added. This one is scoped to a path, which is the whole reason path exists:

    headers:
      # ... the three from lesson 63, still path: /* ...
      # hashed filenames: a name never means different bytes, so never re-ask
      - path: /assets/*
        name: Cache-Control
        value: "public, max-age=31536000, immutable"

31536000 is one year in seconds, which is the maximum anyone respects and the conventional way of writing “forever”. immutable is the interesting half: it tells the browser not to revalidate even when the user hits reload. Without it, a reload re-checks everything; with it, a reload still trusts the cache. That is exactly right for a file whose name is a hash of its own contents.

And you deliberately do not touch index.html. Its current line is already correct: the browser always asks, so a user who reloads gets the new pointer, and the new pointer names the new bundles. Leave s-maxage=300 too — see the honesty box; that is a number to choose on purpose in a later lesson, not to flip today.

The failure this prevents, and the one it creates

Two failure shapes, and you should be able to tell them apart from the symptom alone:

SymptomCause
App looks old after a deploy, correct after five minutes or a reloadA stale index.html, from the CDN. The s-maxage window.
Blank white page, console shows a 404 on a /assets/ fileAn old index.html pointing at bundle names that the new deploy replaced.

The second one is the dangerous one, and it is the reason hashed filenames exist. Both files must be reachable during the window, and this is the part I have not measured on your site: whether Render keeps the previous build’s /assets/ files alive after a new one publishes, or replaces the directory outright. Named in the honesty box, with the check.

Practice — three

Which cache does s-maxage=300 give orders to?

The s is for shared. It is read by caches that serve many users — a CDN or a proxy — and ignored by the browser, which obeys max-age instead. On your site the two numbers say opposite things on purpose: the browser must always ask, and Cloudflare may answer from its copy for five minutes without bothering Render. That five minutes is your post-deploy staleness window.

Why may a hashed bundle be cached for a year?

Vite puts a hash of the contents into the filename, so changed contents always produce a changed name. A cached copy of index-D1HMFRqX.js therefore cannot be out of date — if the code changed, the new HTML asks for a different file entirely. Long caching is safe because the guarantee lives in the name, not in a timer. This is also why index.html gets the opposite treatment: its name is fixed, so it must always be re-checked.

A user sees a blank page right after a deploy. Likely cause?

Their browser is not the culprit: max-age=0 means it re-asks every time. The stale copy is in the shared cache, and the danger is not the staleness itself but the mismatch — an old index.html names bundle files by their old hashes, and if the deploy removed those files each one 404s and nothing renders. The console shows a 404 on a /assets/ path, which is how you tell this apart from a plain “looks old”.

What you did not build, on purpose

left out
unmeasured, and the check is yours whether Render serves the previous deploy’s /assets/ files during the staleness window. Test it: before your next deploy, save a bundle URL from the current page; after the deploy finishes, curl -sI that saved URL. A 200 means old and new coexist and the white-page failure cannot happen. A 404 means the window is real and you should shorten s-maxage. Report the number back.
touching s-maxage on the HTML tempting, and wrong to do blind. Lowering it to 0 removes the staleness window and also removes the CDN — every homepage hit then wakes your Render instance, which on a free plan is the slowest thing in the system. It is a trade between “five minutes stale” and “slower for everyone always”. Decide it with the measurement above in hand.
cache-busting query strings the ?v=2 trick. It is the pre-hash solution to the same problem, and your bundler already solved it properly. Do not add both.
a service worker a third cache, under your own control, with its own update lifecycle and its own class of “why is this user still on the old app” bugs. You do not need offline support. Not now, possibly not ever.
report-uri still waiting on a public unauthenticated route. Its own lesson.
