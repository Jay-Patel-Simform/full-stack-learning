A 404 has an author

You deployed lesson 57 and the page asks GET /api/auth/me. The answer is 404 Not Found. Today you learn to ask who wrote that 404 — and the answer, in your case, is your own API, complaining about a path it was handed. One character is missing from one dashboard field. Zero TypeScript. Suite stays at 172.

In one minute

  • Your rewrite destination is missing its /*. Without the wildcard, every path under /api collapses to / at the API. Add it and the 404 goes away.
  • I measured this, I did not guess it. curl against your live URL returned {"message":"Route GET:/ not found"}. That sentence is written by Fastify, not by Render.
  • A 404 is a signature. The body and the headers tell you which machine answered. A CDN 404 and an app 404 look nothing alike, and they have opposite fixes.
  • The * is two separate jobs. In the source it captures. In the destination it pastes back. You can have the first without the second, and that is exactly the bug.
  • The path shrinks in transit. The browser asks /api/auth/me; your API must see /auth/me, because that is the route you wrote. Two spellings of one request, and one hop between them.

Why — the star does two jobs

One rule, two stars, and they are not the same star.

Source       /api/*        the * here captures  → "auth/me"
Destination  https://api/*  the * here pastes    → "/auth/me"

The source star is a question: does this request start with /api/? It matches, so the rule fires. What it captured is then only used if the destination asks for it. Your destination did not ask. So the rule fired, forwarded the request, and sent the bare destination URL — whose path is /.

Matching and forwarding are two decisions, not one. A rule can match perfectly and still forward the wrong thing. When a proxy misbehaves, always ask both questions separately: did it match, and what did it send?

Why — the 404 told you who wrote it

This is the part worth keeping. There were three possible authors of that 404, and each has a different fix. You can tell them apart without any access to Render's dashboard.

AuthorWhat you would seeWhat it means
Your API (this is yours){"message":"Route GET:/ not found",…} — JSON, plus content-security-policy: default-src 'none' and access-control-allow-credentials: trueThe request arrived. The rewrite works. The path is wrong.

The giveaway in your case was content-security-policy: default-src 'none'. You wrote that line — it is helmet, in server/src/app.ts, lesson 31. A CDN has no reason to send it. The reply carried your fingerprints, so your server had it in its hands.

Every hop leaves marks on the reply. Headers are not decoration; they are a chain of custody. Read them before you change any configuration.

Practice — three

The API replied Route GET:/ not found. What does that prove?

It arrived. Fastify wrote that sentence, and Fastify only runs inside your container — so rule 1 matched and forwarded. What it forwarded was the bare destination, whose path is /. If nothing had matched, Render’s CDN would have answered instead, in HTML, with none of your headers on it.

Which reply means rule 1 never matched at all?

Plain HTML with no content-security-policy and no access-control-allow-credentials means your server never saw the request. Those headers are helmet’s, from app.ts — they are the fingerprints. JSON naming a route is the opposite: that is your app talking.

Why check /api/health rather than /api/auth/me?

/auth/me answers 401 when it is working correctly, because your terminal holds no session cookie. So 401 and “still broken” would look the same. /health needs nothing, so a 200 proves the path arrived and nothing else is in the way.
